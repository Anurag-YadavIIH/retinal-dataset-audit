RetinaPrep: leakage-audit report

Generated by retinaprep report. Self-contained: every image below is inlined as base64. Full methodology in WALKTHROUGH.md, headline copy in README.md, a one-page clinical summary in docs/summary.md.

Training on a naive image-level split (arm A) measures 0.810 AUROC; the correct patient-grouped split (arm B) measures 0.792 -- a +0.018 AUROC gap, same data, same model, same seeds, in the direction the leakage hypothesis predicts. Section 2 below shows why any such gap should be modest even if real (fellow-eye labels only agree 78% of the time, not 100%), and Section 4 shows this particular gap does not replicate significantly at n=5 seeds in a second run -- read both before treating either number as settled.

1. Dataset overview

Descriptive analytics on ODIR-5K itself (6392 images, 3358 patients), before any curation -- demographics, disease prevalence, laterality, and image quality by diagnosis category. Full detail in notebooks/eda.py / artifacts/eda_report.html; the sections below are the same figures, reused directly.

Age distribution

Takeaway: Median age 59, IQR [51, 66] — this is an older, disease-screening population, not a general-population sample; any model trained here should not be assumed to generalise to a younger cohort.

Sex breakdown

Takeaway: Male patients are the majority (53.6%) — close enough to balanced (46.4%/53.6%) that sex is unlikely to be a dominant confound on its own, but not close enough to ignore.

Age by diagnosis category

Takeaway: Cataract patients are oldest (median 67), Normal youngest (median 57) — ages track known clinical patterns (cataract/AMD skew older, myopia skews younger), which is a sanity check that these labels behave the way real diagnoses should.

Disease prevalence

Takeaway: Normal is the most common pathology (1080 patients), Hypertension the rarest (103) — a >10x spread between disease categories that a multi-class or per-disease task (assessed but not run this project) would need to handle explicitly, e.g. with class weighting, or it will under-serve the rare categories.

Comorbidity

Takeaway: 556 patients (16.6%) carry 2+ disease categories simultaneously — comorbidity is common enough that a binary normal/abnormal task, as used in this project's main experiment, is collapsing real clinical heterogeneity. Diabetic retinopathy+Other is the most common specific pairing (281 patients).

Laterality by disease category

Takeaway: Hypertension is the most bilateral condition (89%), Other the least (20%) — this is the direct, per-disease breakdown behind the project's 77.8% overall concordance finding: systemic conditions affect both eyes together far more than localised ones, so leaking a patient leaks more label information for some diseases than others.

Single-eye vs two-eye patients

Takeaway: Single-eye patients differ significantly from two-eye patients on: diagnosis rate (age p=0.3124, sex p=0.8306, diagnosis-rate p=0.0000). Diagnosis rate is higher for single-eye patients (81.8% vs 66.3%) — the missing eye is not random, which is a real selection bias worth naming: whatever caused that eye to be unavailable correlates with who these 324 patients are.

Raw image resolution

Takeaway: 41 distinct resolutions in a 300-image sample, ranging 1280-5184px wide — a real mixed-camera dataset. This is exactly why quality.py resizes to a fixed size before blur metrics (moot for the pipeline's actual input, `preprocessed_images/`, which is uniformly 512x512, but live for this folder).

Quality score by diagnosis category

Takeaway: Cataract images do score lower (median 0.874 vs 0.877 for normal) — the mechanistically expected direction (cataract causes media opacity), even though abnormal-vs-normal *overall* showed no such gap (docs/notes.md). Glaucoma has the lowest median quality score (0.844) of any category — worth checking specifically before assuming quality curation is disease-blind at the category level, even though it looked that way for the coarse binary label.

Class imbalance

Takeaway: 55/45 split is mild, not severe, imbalance -- accuracy alone would still be a misleading metric, which is why this project reports AUROC/AUPRC/sensitivity-at-specificity rather than accuracy, and why the train/val/test split is stratified on label throughout.

2. Leakage-audit findings

The pipeline's headline result: how much measured performance changes under different splitting and curation regimes, and why. Full detail in notebooks/findings_charts.py / artifacts/findings_report.html.

Patient overlap across split strategies

Takeaway: 1412/3358 patients (42.0%) land on both sides of image_random -- by construction, 0% do under patient_group. This is the simplest and most important chart in this project: it is a count, not a statistic, and needs no significance test.

Fellow-eye label concordance

Takeaway: Fellow eyes share the same normal/abnormal label 77.8% of the time, against a 50.5% floor from class balance alone -- real bilateral correlation, but far short of 100%. This is why the ~42% patient overlap shown above produces a modest ~0.017 AUROC effect rather than something dramatic: leaking a patient's identity leaks a noisy, ~78%-reliable hint about their label, not the label itself.

4-arm results

Takeaway: B (the correct split) scores 0.792 AUROC vs A's 0.810 -- leakage inflates the naive split, as predicted. C is +0.003 vs B (the surprising result: quality curation *cost* AUROC, sign-consistent across all 5 seeds -- see docs/notes.md for why the flattering explanation didn't survive being checked). D is -0.006 vs B, consistent with the predicted null for a curation stage removing <1% of images.

A-vs-B: does the gap replicate?

Takeaway: Run 1 (cap=4473): A-B=+0.0173, p=0.037. Run 2 (cap=4435): A-B=+0.0079, p=0.286. Run 3 (uncapped, post split-then-curate fix): A-B=+0.0183, p=0.053. Runs 1 and 3 cluster near +0.018; Run 2 sits lower at +0.008 -- none reaches significance except Run 1's uncorrected p, and none survives Bonferroni correction across the family this project holds every claim to. Three draws, one direction, no draw individually conclusive: this is what n=5 seeds being underpowered looks like when actually re-drawn twice, not argued from theory once.

The three-rung hierarchy: A vs B vs E

Takeaway: A (image_random, 0.810) vs B (patient_group, 0.792): +0.018 AUROC (A-B) -- real but small, and did not replicate significantly across three independent 5-seed runs (chart above). B vs E (site_group, 0.737): -0.055 AUROC (E-B) -- over 3x larger, 5/5 seeds agreeing, Bonferroni-significant (p=0.0026). Not a like-for-like comparison of test sets: E's test fold is a single held-out site with 63% abnormal prevalence vs B's 55% -- checked directly (docs/notes.md) and the drop survives prevalence-matching within 6% of its own size, so this is not a prevalence artifact. Each rung's split catches leakage the rung above it structurally cannot.

Leave-one-site-out: is E's drop one site or a real pattern?

Takeaway: 4/5 held-out sites show the same negative gap as the original arm E result (site_0) -- this is not just the one site that happened to land in the original split. site_4 is a genuine exception (+0.021, consistent across all 3 of its own seeds, not a training fluke) that class balance and embedding-distance-from-training don't explain (docs/notes.md). Every interval shown here spans zero, including site_0's -- accounting for test-fold size (not just seed variance) shows no single site's estimate is precise enough alone to rule out a true gap of zero; the sweep's value is the consistent direction across sites, not any one site's number read in isolation.

Training-set overlap falsification test

Takeaway: Removing the 43 quality-rejected images and re-splitting gives 69.2% overlap with B's original training set; removing 43 *random*, quality-uncorrelated images gives 69.4% -- statistically indistinguishable. This is the falsification test for the flattering hypothesis (curation removed informative-but-hard-to-grade images): it isn't about which images are removed, it's that StratifiedGroupKFold reshuffles ~31% of the training set from almost any small change to its input pool.

Embedding similarity: fellow eyes vs duplicates

Takeaway: Fellow-eye pairs: mean 0.934, max 0.987 (n=3034). Confirmed duplicates: mean 0.993, min 0.962 (n=11). The threshold (0.99) separates them almost completely -- 0 fellow-eye pairs sit at or above it -- but not perfectly: 1 confirmed duplicate(s) fall below it, which is exactly why dedupe.py also needs phash as an independent signal rather than relying on embeddings alone.

3. Quality-control panels

Every verified duplicate is a cross-patient integrity issue: the same photograph filed under two different declared patient IDs, not two images of one patient. Patient-grouped splitting has no mechanism to catch this -- it only keeps a single declared ID's images together, so deduplication is closing a leakage gap that splitting structurally cannot (docs/notes.md).

Class balance before/after curation

Class balance moves only slightly across curation stages (quality removes 43 images, dedupe at most 22 more) -- curation is not manufacturing arm C/D's results by shifting what the model sees at the class level; the 43 rejected images are 62.8% normal, close to the dataset's own baseline, not disproportionately abnormal.
