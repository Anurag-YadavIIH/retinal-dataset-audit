RetinaPrep: retinal fundus leakage audit

Fundus datasets leak at three levels, and each level of protection is insufficient for the next — measured end to end on two independent datasets: ODIR-5K (6,392 images, multi-centre Chinese) and EyePACS (35,126 images, a US telemedicine screening network).

The third row is the one to read twice: patient-grouped splitting — what most papers mean by "we split properly" — cannot catch one photograph filed under two patient IDs. In EyePACS, 58% of duplicate clusters straddle a patient-grouped split anyway.

Duplicate counts are measured at matched sample size (6,392 images each) so the two columns are comparable — the EyePACS figure is not a dataset total. The full-dataset count is higher and unmeasured, and does not scale linearly: candidate pairs grow quadratically with dataset size.

Stated up front rather than buried: the downstream AUROC cost of level-1 leakage is small and did not replicate cleanly; "site" is a resolution-derived proxy, not camera metadata, in both datasets; every individual held-out site's confidence interval spans zero; and the duplicate finding shows contamination exists, not that any specific published result was affected. Full write-up, headline numbers and methodology are in the GitHub repository's README and WALKTHROUGH.md.

A fourth strand: what the labels themselves are worth

The three levels above are one argument, about splits, and every result in them is measured against labels. A fourth strand runs alongside all three rather than after them, and asks what those labels are worth. Segmentation is where that question has a clean answer, because a few datasets ship the same images graded twice by different people.

The last row is the one with a clinical consequence. Disc localisation degrades as exudate burden rises, so it is least reliable on exactly the eyes a screening programme exists to find, and an aggregate Dice will not show it because florid eyes are a minority of any test set. Disc outlining is normally a preprocessing step — cup-to-disc ratio, centring, registration — so the failure propagates into whatever is computed next.

Stated up front here too: n is 20, 27 and 28 images, which is how large the public dual-graded datasets are, and that was declared before the runs rather than after. DRIVE's second observer comes from a third-party re-upload whose structure was verified but whose chain of custody was not — CHASE_DB1 carries no such caveat and gives the same ceiling, which is why two datasets were used. One prediction made in advance failed: the model agrees more with the observer it never trained on, and that reversal is reported rather than dropped. One of the three disc failures remains unexplained.
